Course agenda

Regional Training Course on Experimental Design and Data Analysis for the Advancement of Mutant Populations (Project RER5030), Skopje, North Macedonia, 9 to 20 November 2026.

Venue: Faculty of Agricultural Sciences and Food, Ss Cyril and Methodius University in Skopje, blvd “16-ta Makedonska brigada” no. 3, 1000 Skopje.

Revised agenda, version 7, 7 November 2026. Times are local time in Skopje. Chapters for the later sessions are added as the course advances.

Trainers: Mirjana Jankulovska (local organizer and expert, Faculty of Agricultural Sciences and Food, Skopje) and Luis Fernando Delgado Muñoz (international expert, Universidad San Buenaventura, Colombia). Technical officers: Cinthya Zorrilla and Pooja Mathur, FAO/IAEA Centre of Nuclear Techniques in Food and Agriculture.

Day 2, Tuesday, 10 November 2026: Experimental design and ANOVA

Time Session Lead
08:30 - 09:00 The Mutant Variety Database: how to use it and new features Cinthya Zorrilla

• CRD, RCBD, augmented, alpha-lattice, row-column and resolvable row-column designs
• Partially replicated (p-rep) designs for early generations with limited seed (one plant in some cases)
• Non-replicated trial designs for early generations with limited seed
• Blocking and error control
• Design selection by breeding stage (single row, PYT, AYT; cassava case); seed crop examples (bean, rice)
• Preview: design considerations for early-generation mutant populations		 Luis Fernando Delgado
10:00 - 10:30 Break
10:30 - 12:30 Practical: Design Generation, Field Layouts and Model Structure
• Randomization and design creation with FieldHub
• Export of field layouts and field books
• Interpretation of layouts: factors, replications, sources of variation
• Model terms derived from the layout (treatment, block, row, column)		 Luis Fernando Delgado
12:30 - 13:30 Lunch

• Model fitting in R

Day 4, Thursday, 12 November 2026: Spatial analysis and low-cost high-throughput phenotyping

Time Session Lead
08:30 - 10:00 Lecture + Demo: Spatial Variation and Trial Precision
• Field heterogeneity with didactic cases: tree lines along a road, trials divided by a highway, fields crossed by a river, soil moisture gradients
• Residual patterns from mixed models and justification for spatial correction
• Design vs statistical correction		 Luis Fernando Delgado
10:00 - 10:30 Break
10:30 - 12:30 Practical: Spatial Analysis
• Spatial model fitting with SpATS; Mr.Bean Shiny app
• Spatial vs non-spatial comparison: precision, heritability and ranking changes		 Luis Fernando Delgado
12:30 - 13:30 Lunch
13:30 - 14:45 Lecture: Low-Cost High-Throughput Phenotyping with Imaging
• Low-cost equipment: smartphone and RGB cameras, modified near-infrared cameras
• Image acquisition protocols: lighting, background, scale and colour references
• Traits from images: disease lesion area and severity, leaf colour change (chlorosis, senescence), colour and vegetation indices, canopy cover, seed and fruit size and colour
• Validating image-based traits against visual scores; repeatability and heritability of image traits		 Luis Fernando Delgado
14:45 - 15:00 Break
15:00 - 16:30 Practical: Image Analysis in R
• Measuring disease severity and leaf colour from RGB images (pliman package)
• Batch processing of images and linking results to the field book
• Using image-derived traits in the mixed model: comparison with visual scores		 Luis Fernando Delgado
16:30 - 17:00 Q&A and Discussion: imaging setups in participants’ programs Luis Fernando Delgado

Day 5, Friday, 13 November 2026: G×E, multi-environment trials and machine learning

Time Session Lead
08:30 - 10:15 Lecture: G×E Interaction and Multi-Environment Trials
• Limitations of single-environment evaluation
• Stability vs adaptability; mega-environments
• Foundations of PCA with a simple toy example		 Mirjana Jankulovska
10:15 - 10:45 Break
10:45 - 12:30 Practical: Multi-Environment Trial Analysis
• Data structure and model fitting
• Genotype performance across environments		 Mirjana Jankulovska
12:30 - 13:30 Lunch
13:30 - 14:45 Lecture + Practical: Stability, AMMI and GGE Biplot
• Stability indices
• AMMI and GGE biplot interpretation
• Integration of performance and stability in selection		 Mirjana Jankulovska
14:45 - 15:00 Break
15:00 - 16:15 Lecture: Machine Learning for Prediction in Plant Breeding
• When machine learning improves prediction: large datasets from imaging, NIRS, multi-environment trials and environmental covariates
• Main models: penalized regression (ridge, LASSO, elastic net), random forest, gradient boosting, support vector machines; PLS for NIRS spectra
• Applications: predicting traits from image features and spectra, disease severity, yield and G×E prediction
• Validation: training and test sets, cross-validation across genotypes and environments, avoiding data leakage, prediction accuracy
• Limitations: small datasets, overfitting, interpretability, transfer across crops, environments and seasons; breeder knowledge remains essential		 Luis Fernando Delgado
16:15 - 17:00 Week 1 wrap-up and dataset check-in
• Q&A session
• Short review quiz
• Each participant confirms the dataset and analysis plan for week 2		 All experts

Week 2

Day 6, Monday, 16 November 2026: Analysis pipeline, machine learning and mutation breeding

Time Session Lead
08:30 - 10:15 Demonstration: End-to-End Data Analysis Pipeline
• Case examples with cassava and bean breeding data
• Raw data → quality control → model → spatial correction → BLUPs → heritability → selection
• Quantitative and qualitative traits
• Reproducible, script-based workflows in R		 Luis Fernando Delgado
10:15 - 10:45 Break
10:45 - 12:30 Practical: Machine Learning Prediction Models in R
• Predicting a trait from image-derived features (Day 4) and NIRS spectra
• Comparing linear models, elastic net, random forest and gradient boosting with cross-validation
• Variable importance and interpretation
• When machine learning outperforms classical models, and when it does not		 Luis Fernando Delgado
12:30 - 13:30 Lunch
13:30 - 15:00 Practical: Radiosensitivity Analysis (Topic 10)
• Dose-response analysis using measurements taken on each plant (for example plant height or weight)
• LD50 vs GR50: why mutation breeding uses growth reduction rather than lethality
• Choosing the best dose: enough damage to create variability, with plants that survive
• Mutation breeding follows conventional breeding; only the way variability is produced differs		 Luis Fernando Delgado
15:00 - 15:15 Break
15:15 - 16:15 Lecture: Starting a Mutation Breeding Programme (Topic 10)
• Generations M1 to M5: what is evaluated in each (growth, size, number of leaves, weight)
• Mutations affect a single copy of the genome: recessive and dominant effects, and how the mutated allele is revealed
• Crop biology (autogamous and allogamous) and its consequences for the programme		 Mirjana Jankulovska
16:15 - 17:00 Statistical Pipelines for Mutation Breeding Applications (Topic 10)
• Analysis of mutant populations (M2 to M5)
• Selection under high variability (unreplicated and replicated approaches at early generations)
• Application of statistical pipelines to mutation breeding of different types of crops (autogamous and allogamous)		 Luis Fernando Delgado
