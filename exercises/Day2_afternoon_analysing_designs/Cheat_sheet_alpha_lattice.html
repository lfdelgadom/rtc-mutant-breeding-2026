Alpha-lattice cheat sheet

RER5030, Day 2. One page, plain words.

1. The idea in one sentence

An RCBD puts all entries in one big block. When there are many entries, that block is too large to be uniform. An alpha-lattice cuts each replicate into small blocks, so each entry is compared with neighbours in a small, more uniform area.

4. Two words

  • Incomplete block: a block that holds only some of the entries (4 of 24), not all.
  • Resolvable: the blocks are grouped in replicates, and every replicate contains all entries. So you can still analyse the replicates as complete blocks if you need to.

5. Reading the model

yield ~ rep + (1 | rep:block) + gen
genthe entries: what we want to compare (fixed term, gives the means)
repreplicates: differences between the three big areas
(1 | rep:block)incomplete blocks: removes the variation between small blocks (random term)
residualwhat remains: the variance that the model cannot explain
Why rep:block and not block? Block numbers repeat in every replicate: B1 of rep 1 is a different piece of land than B1 of rep 2. rep:block says "block B1 within replicate R1". Using only block would wrongly join pieces of land that are far apart.

6. Does it pay? What to look at

  • Residual variance: smaller with the alpha-lattice model than with the RCBD model.
  • SED (standard error of a difference between two entry means): smaller, so the same difference becomes easier to detect. LSD ≈ 2 x SED.
  • If the residual variance does not change, the blocks did not catch field variation, and the extra term was not needed.

7. In FielDHub

library(FielDHub)
d <- alpha_lattice(t = 24, k = 4, r = 3, seed = 2026)
head(d$fieldBook)     # columns: REP, IBLOCK, UNIT, ENTRY, TREATMENT
plot(d)
t must be divisible by k. For t = 24 you can use k = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12. Not k = 5: FielDHub stops with "Combinations for this amount of treatments do not exist". For 30 entries: k = 3, 5, 6, 10.

8. Choosing k

  • Smaller k: more uniform blocks, but more blocks to manage.
  • Typical in breeding: k between 4 and 10.
  • The block should match something real in the field (a few neighbouring rows, a part of a slope).

9. Four questions before sowing

  1. How many entries (t) and how many plots do I have?
  2. Which k divides t and fits a uniform piece of land?
  3. How many replicates (r)? With limited seed, r = 2.
  4. Do I keep the row and the column of every plot in the field book? (You need them for the analysis.)
